# Encoders, evaluation sets, pre-embeddings and graphs for the French, German and EU collections (2026-10-28)

**What this is.** The research behind
[`legal-fr-de-eu.md`](legal-fr-de-eu.md) §8's decisions D3 and D4.

The operator's words, 2026-10-28:
- D3: "one encoder per language if it fits, find proper embedders like
  DAR legal it … find a proper embedder for the main 4 languages
  proposed, for the others use a compromise so far".
- D4: "do not reinvent the wheel, find papers, we already have
  something".
- Also: "Check also the existence of pre embeddings and of graphs".

**Trust.** Three research passes on the evening of 2026-10-28. **[V]**
means the fact was read on the primary page: a model or dataset card,
or the paper through the Hugging Face paper mirror. **[U]** means it is
second-hand. This session could not reach `arxiv.org`, `isaacus.com`,
`op.europa.eu` or `wikidata.org`, so facts from those sites are [U].
Nothing here is pinned before it is measured on the node (§5).

**What "we already have" is.** `tools/encoder_bakeoff.py`, the harness
that chose DAR-legal-it on JuriFindIT's 179 expert questions. It is
proven, and its metric code is pinned by tests. Its runs are compared
with each other on the same corpus, the same truncation and the same
code. §5 makes it read the sets below.

embedder trained on French national statutes with expert questions. A
search of the Hub for `legal-fr`, `juridique`, `droit`, `french-legal`,

There is no expert set on French NATIONAL statutes. BSARD (Belgian law,
French language, expert-labelled) is the nearest, and it is the one
MTEB-French carries.

**Pre-embeddings — they exist.** `AgentPublic/legi` (etalab-2.0, 2.2 M
rows, 11.9 GB) embeds every LEGI article with **bge-m3** [V]:
- Unit: one article, split above 1024 bge-m3 tokens, no overlap.
- Text embedded: `"{full_title} - Article {number}\n{subtitles}\n{text}"`.
- Coverage: every `ETAT` (in force, modified, repealed …), snapshots
  roughly every two weeks from 2025-09-05 to 2026-09-26, plus `latest`.
- The vectors are stored as JSON strings.

Siblings use the same pipeline: `AgentPublic/dole`, `constit`, `cnil`,
`service-public`, `travail-emploi`. There is no JORF, KALI or case-law
set, and no pre-embedded French EUR-Lex [V]. `ArthurSrz/open_codes` uses
`mistral-embed`, an API-only model, so it is unusable (invariant 7).

**Graphs — one is ready-made.** `AgentPublic/legi` has a `links` column
per article [V]:
- fields: `link_type` (TXT_SOURCE, CITATION, …), the direction, the
  target LEGI/JORF id, the NOR and the signature date;
- coverage: all of LEGI, etalab-2.0, Parquet.

The raw LEGI `<LIENS>` also carry MODIFIE, ABROGE, CODIFICATION and
TRANSFERE [U]. Our parser keeps those anyway (§2.4 of the design).
Research graphs (Mazzega–Bourcier–Boulet's inter-code citations; the
work of Mimouni et al.) released no data [U]. FiscalQA Pro releases
69,208 version-aware links from case law to articles [U].

## 2. German — NeuRIS (+ EUR-Lex in German)

**Legal embedders now exist with licences; a statute retriever does not.**
Since 2026 there are Apache-2.0 legal embedders trained on German data.
None was trained on statutes and chosen on statute questions.
`litil-embed-0.6b` alone has statutes in its training: 2,150 rows of
heading → §.

Two findings that shape the measurement [V]:
- **No MTEB task measures German statute retrieval.** GerDaLIRSmall and

  the best on LegalQuAD (76.8 against 77.2). BM25 is therefore a

## 4. What this changes in the design

- **France:**
  - **RULE 1 can apply.** If bge-m3 wins or ties on BSARD, Syntec and
    FiscalQA, AgentPublic's vectors are an archive layer to carry. Kept
    where a sample re-embedded on our engine agrees within the
    tolerance, recomputed where not: Wikipedia's Upstash path, with
    `--calibration-from`.
  - **Its unit must be ours.** Their embedded text form (title, number,
    subtitles, text) is fixed, so the LEGI plugin's text form either
    matches it byte for byte or the vectors are not carried.
- **France, links:** AgentPublic's `links` column is a check, not a
  source. Our records carry LEGI's `<LIENS>` themselves, and the two
  are compared on a sample.
- **Germany:** no vectors to carry. BM25 must be a baseline: it beats
  every dense model on GerDaLIRSmall.
- **Germany, history:** `legal-networks/gesetze-im-internet` holds GII's
  daily versions since 2020; not used (operator, "no"). The history before
  our first snapshot comes from NeuRIS as it publishes it.
- **EU:** the graph comes from the CDM per act; EvoEU is a historical
  cross-check.

## 5. The measurement — the bake-off, widened

`tools/encoder_bakeoff.py` reads JuriFindIT's own layout. Every set above
that MTEB or MLEB carries is published in the **BEIR layout** (Thakur et
al., NeurIPS 2021: `corpus`, `queries`, `qrels`), the standard for
retrieval evaluation:
- BSARD, Syntec, GerDaLIR and LegalQuAD (`mteb/…`);
- MLEB's sets (`isaacus/…`).

So the bake-off learns one reader, the BEIR layout, and not one reader
per set. Its metric code, which is pinned by tests, stays as it is.
**Built (2026-10-28):**

    python -m tools.encoder_bakeoff fetch --suite bsard --out /data/bakeoff-bsard
    python -m tools.encoder_bakeoff embed --out /data/bakeoff-bsard --name bge-m3 --url … --model bge-m3
    python -m tools.encoder_bakeoff score --out /data/bakeoff-bsard
    python -m tools.encoder_bakeoff report --out /data/bakeoff-bsard

- Suites: `jurifindit`, `bsard`, `syntec`, `legalquad`, `gerdalir-small`
  and `gdpr-holdings`. Each directory holds one set.
- The report shows nDCG@10 and MRR@10 beside the bake-off's own columns.

**What Louise serves is the hybrid, and that is what is measured.** A
search fuses the word leg (tantivy, BM25, the language's stemmer) with the
vector leg by RRF, then reranks; it never answers with vectors alone.

    python -m tools.encoder_bakeoff lexical --out /data/bakeoff-bsard --lang fr

- `lexical` ranks the set with `app/fts.py` itself: a fresh index built
  by `fts.index_books`, each question through `fts.search`. No BM25
  library stands in for it.
- `score` then fuses each encoder's ranking with it through
  `retrieval._merge_rrf`, the search's own fusion (k = 60, its depth, its
  tie rule).
- The report has three rows per encoder: **BM25 (Louise)**, **⟨encoder⟩**
  and **⟨encoder⟩ + BM25 (RRF)**.

**What the node serves (2026-10-29).** Every GPU encoder is VeGa-wrapped
(`vega-bge-m3`, `vega-qwen3-embedding-4b`, …). **Qwen3-Embedding-8B is not
served; the 4B is.** The 8B rows wait for a deploy of it, so the compromise
is measured as the 4B first. The bake-off asks a `vega-…` model through the
app's tagging client; its first GPU runs were refused for sending untagged
inputs.

**First number: BM25 (Louise) on LegalQuAD, nDCG@10 0.7618** against
MTEB's BM25 76.8. Our word leg is MTEB's BM25, within 0.6 point.

**LegalQuAD (German court decisions, 200 questions; München 2026-10-29,
main 09e61a5).** MRR@10 / nDCG@10:

| row | alone | + BM25 (RRF) |
|---|---|---|
| BM25 (Louise) | 0.7258 / 0.7618 | — |
| vega-bge-m3 | 0.4861 / 0.5383 | **0.6212** / 0.6704 — below BM25 alone |
| vega-qwen3-embedding-4b, bare | 0.6530 / 0.6924 | **0.7457** / 0.7787 |
| the same, Instruct prefix | 0.6556 / 0.6958 | 0.7345 / 0.7733 |

What it shows so far:
- **A weak vector leg pulls the hybrid below BM25 alone.** bge-m3
  costs 10.5 points of MRR@10. The encoder decides whether fusion
  helps or hurts.
- The 4B's hybrid is +2.0 points over BM25. The Instruct prefix changes
  nothing measurable.
- **Two points on 200 questions is within noise** (per-query spread
  ~0.4). The bake-off therefore now gives a paired randomization test
  per row against BM25, and the leader against every other row
  (`paired`, Smucker et al. CIKM 2007), before any of these is a
  choice.
- The set is case law, not statutes. It ranks encoders on German legal
  language; it does not choose NeuRIS's.

**The paired test (MRR@10, 200 questions, Fisher randomization, 95%
bootstrap):**

| comparison | Δ | interval | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3-4b + BM25 vs BM25 | +0.020 | [−0.025, +0.067] | 0.40, **a tie** |
| bge-m3 + BM25 vs BM25 | −0.105 | [−0.159, −0.050] | 0.0005, **worse** |
| qwen3-4b + BM25 vs qwen3-4b alone | +0.093 | [+0.056, +0.131] | 0.0001, **fusion helps** |
| qwen3-4b bare vs Instruct (both fused) | +0.011 | [−0.007, +0.030] | 0.25, a tie |
| qwen3-4b alone vs BM25 | −0.073 | [−0.131, −0.015] | 0.014 |

**On German court decisions the best hybrid ties word search, and a weak
encoder makes it worse.** That is the reason the rule chooses on the
hybrid, and why bge-m3 is out for German law.

**GerDaLIRSmall: not run on the card.**

| choice | effect | pros | cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **skip it** (chosen) | the card goes to statute sets (LEMUR now; BSARD and EUR-Lex-Sum after their acknowledgement) | the sets that choose a STATUTE encoder | no own number for German case law with long documents |
| run it | ~40 min for bge-m3, ~3.5–4 h for qwen3-4b (München's projection) | our own row beside MTEB's | case law with citation-derived questions, already answered by MTEB (BM25 59.4 against ≤ 54.7 dense); the card is shared with the corpus |

`--limit` now cuts the questions as well as the documents. A GerDaLIRSmall
smoke embedded all 12,234 of them.

**LEMUR, en / fr / de / it** (EU environmental acts; the query is the act's
metadata header; about 225 acts each; one row per act; München 2026-10-29,
main fd74d32). MRR@10, and the paired p against BM25:

| row | en | fr | de | it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BM25 (Louise) | 0.864 | 0.888 | 0.860 | 0.862 |
| vega-qwen3-embedding-4b | **0.934** (p 0.0002) | 0.914 (0.10) | **0.903** (0.024) | 0.886 (0.19) |
| qwen3-4b + BM25 | 0.901 (0.0004) | **0.916** (0.0066) | 0.895 (0.0014) | 0.874 (0.24) |
| vega-bge-m3 | 0.775 | 0.790 | 0.730 | 0.790 |
| bge-m3 + BM25 | 0.851 (0.44) | 0.871 (0.28) | 0.829 (0.042, worse) | 0.842 (0.16) |

**What the five sets say together** (LegalQuAD and the four LEMURs):
- **bge-m3 is out.** Alone it loses to BM25 everywhere, and fused it never
  wins and twice loses.
- **The 4B fused never loses to BM25**, and wins significantly on three of
  the five sets (LEMUR en, fr, de).
- **In English the 4B alone beats its own fusion** (+0.032, p 0.005). A
  vector leg much stronger than the word leg is diluted by RRF. That is the
  reranker's job to repair, and worth measuring once the reranker row
  exists.
- **The caution: LEMUR is a title-shaped task.** The query is the act's
  header (title, date, number), recall@100 is about 1.0, and BM25 already
  reaches 0.86. It ranks encoders on finding an EU act by its description,
  which a legal question is not. It is the green evidence, not the
  deciding one.

**DAR-legal-it on `lemur-it`** (Normattiva's live encoder, `vega-dar-legal-it`,
ctx 8192). MRR@10 against BM25 0.862:
- served form (`query: `, Normattiva's instruction): 0.879 (p 0.40);
  fused: 0.863 (p 0.92);
- bare: 0.872; fused: 0.863.
- Against the 4B: +0.0075 for the 4B (p 0.64), a tie.

**EU-it in Normattiva's space costs nothing measurable here.** DAR-legal-it
was chosen on JuriFindIT's expert questions, which include EU acts in
Italian. So D3's "Italian = Normattiva's space + EU-it" stands.

**München's finding:** the report said a bare row "is what this node
serves". For a space with a query instruction it is not: Normattiva serves
`query: ` + the question. `embed --from-collection <name>` now takes the
collection's own recipe (`recipe_for`: url, model, api, ctx, instruction),
and the app prefixes the instruction as a search does. The false sentence
is gone from the report.

**So far:** Qwen3-Embedding-4B is the compromise for the EU languages
without a legal encoder. A legal encoder must beat the 4B's hybrid at
p < 0.01 to replace it. Next rows:
- DAR-legal-it on `lemur-it` (EU-it shares Normattiva's space);
- the French and German legal candidates once served;
- BSARD and EUR-Lex-Sum once acknowledged.

**The rule:** an encoder is chosen on its HYBRID MRR@10 — what it adds to
the word leg — with ties broken by cost (size, dimensions) — and a tie is what the paired test cannot separate at p < 0.01. The reranker
